Entering New Eden and the Formation of a Sandbox Society, 2003-2006

In the early twenty-first century, an Icelandic game company launched
what seemed to be just another massive multiplayer online game. EVE
Online , launched in May 2003, featured spaceships, resource production,
economic trade, and territorial warfare. But the element that
distinguished EVE Online from other online games was not the game modes,
but rather the way in which they were realized. Unlike other MMOsand
persistent world games of the time, EVE Online did not allow players to
separate themselves from one another. The universe of New Eden was a
single shared environment, where each player had access to the same
opportunities and dangers. There were no dividing walls, no separated
servers, and no distinct worlds for each player to experience
individually.

This feature shaped the development of the game world in significant
ways. The majority of online games at the time were fairly restricted in
their design, offering limited opportunities for inter-player
interaction and imposing strict limitations on the ways in which players
could engage with one another. Even the most open-ended persistent world
games featured strict design constraints, limiting the ways in which
players could band together, form organizations, and engage in economic
or social interactions. EVE Online development team created an open
world, which allowed players to shape the game world around their
individual actions and decisions. As a result, players engaged in all
manner of economic and social experiments, forming unique organizations,
institutions, markets, and alliances. The game design itself did not
dictate the rules of these interactions, leaving them to emerge
independently within the game world.

The accounts from the early years of New Eden illustrate a world fraught
with suspicion and uncertainty. New players quickly learned that the
game did not offer many protections for the weak and vulnerable. Outside
the protected areas of high-security space, players could expect anyone
to remove their ship, strip their cargo, and take their wealth with
little to no risk for the aggressor. Worse still, the actions of other
players were rarely punished by the administrators. Many of the largest
organizations at the time were created precisely to address this
concern, offering their members economic opportunities and security, but
only at the cost of strict loyalty and obedience to the organizations'
directors.

The early years of New Eden demonstrate the ways in which trust and
suspicion shaped the development of institutions within the game.
Large-scale organizations had limited opportunities to engage their
members directly, which resulted in the development of complex screening
and recruitment procedures. In order to mitigate the risks associated
with untrustworthy members, corporations and alliances alike were forced
to implement elaborate security measures. The themes of trust and
security shaped the development of the organizational memory of New
Eden, influencing the ways in which institutions interacted with one
another. The reputation of an entity within New Eden was an essential
factor in determining its success, and the institutions of New Eden had
to develop methods to assess the reputations of their potential members.

Player-run organizations of New Eden had to develop elaborate trust and
security protocols because the game itself rarely protected its players
from one another. One of the most prominent illustrations of this
phenomenon was the infamous scam that rocked the player community in the
early years of the game. One unscrupulous player designed an intricate
scheme, tricking thousands of other players into depositing vast sums of
money into his account. While he ultimately got punished by CCP Games
for his misdeeds, the punishment was not enough to prevent this player
from continuing his work as a scam artist. More importantly, the scam
incident served as a lesson for many players, who realized just how
little security the game provided in terms of player-to-player
interactions. The scam lived on in the cultural memory of New Eden,
inspiring countless conspiracy theories and fueling the paranoia of many
players.

Trust and betrayal lived at the heart of early New Eden, shaping the
development of many institutions and interactions within the game world.
The themes of reputation and integrity shaped the institutional memory
of New Eden, helping to determine the fates of many organizations. The
large-scale organizations were formed to help regular players navigate
the dangerous environment of New Eden, but with the introduction of
formal screening procedures, the process of joining such organizations
became significantly more difficult. The corporations and alliances of
New Eden had to develop complex management systems to ensure the loyalty
of their members. In return, many players demanded more transparency
from their organizations, which further shaped the evolution of
institutional security within New Eden. Many of these themes stemmed
from the environment of distrust, which persisted throughout the early
years of the game.

As organizations grew, they needed stronger leadership to ensure their
prosperity and survival. The most prominent figures of the early New
Eden were not warriors or conquerors of any particular region or system.
Instead, many of them were administrators, who managed their
directorates with a mix of discipline and oversight. The very existence
of large-scale organizations diminished the role of traditional power
dynamics of the online game world. Organizations such as corporations
and alliances acted as stabilizing forces, which ensured the survival of
their members. They maximized the rewards of play while minimizing the
risks, serving as strong economic and military entities. As a result,
the early New Eden saw the rise of a unique political order, which was
based on preparation, administration, and long-term planning.

The early years of New Eden illustrated the ways in which large-scale
institutions minimized the risks associated with unorganized play. They
created stable economic and military structures within the game, making
the survival within New Eden significantly easier for their members.
Many of these organizations became so influential that they actively
shaped the development of the game itself. As players continued to
engage in organized play, the next chapter of New Eden's history was set
to explore the intricacies of institutional development and growth.

Growth and the Development of Institutions, 2006-2009

### The Expansion of New Eden's Organizations

The early years of New Eden saw the birth and subsequent rise of many
diverse organizations, each trying to carve its niche and secure its
place in New Eden's increasingly competitive environment. The
corporations and alliances of New Eden were growing stronger by the day,
utilizing their resources and connections to maximize the gains and
minimize the losses of their members. But as these organizations grew
stronger and more influential, they also became more impersonal,
shifting away from personal connections and relying on formal procedures
and restrictions to maintain order and security.

Trust became a valuable resource, one that had to be obtained, guarded,
and occasionally exploited. Organizations had to operate under the
assumption of a limited and suspect trust economy, implementing strict
screening procedures to ensure the loyalty of potential members. As many
accounts from the early years of New Eden suggest, a single disloyal or
careless individual could deal a severe blow to any organization,
especially if they held influential positions within it. Such breaches
of trust could erode an organization's security, undermine its economy,
and damage its reputation, with lasting consequences for all of its
members.

### The Rise of Espionage

Some of the most notable institutions and practices of New Eden were the
result of this competitive and distrustful environment. The practice of
espionage became especially popular and effective, as it allowed
organizations to gain valuable intel on their rivals and competitors.
Spying was a lucrative activity, one that allowed organizations to
bypass many of the restrictions and limitations of direct engagement.
The most effective spies were those who operated under the protection of
their target, deceiving their opponents and subsequently stealing
valuable data, resources, or even personnel.

The practice was especially prevalent within New Eden's organizations,
which relied on secrecy, security, and strict screening procedures to
maintain their influence and power. Consequently, spies who managed to
infiltrate these organizations often found themselves rewarded for their
services, receiving promotions, bonuses, and other rewards from their
employers. At the same time, successful espionage operations served as
potent cautionary tales, acting as grim reminders of the potential
vulnerabilities within the organizations' security infrastructure.

Espionage and the culture of secrecy that surrounded it became integral
parts of New Eden's institutional memory. Events surrounding betrayal
and exposure tended to live on in the collective consciousness of the
game's citizens, fueling speculation and paranoia long after their
initial occurrence. The memory of espionage incidents served as powerful
cautionary tales that influenced the decision-making processes of many
New Eden residents. As a result, organizations invested a large amount
of time and effort into updating and improving their
intelligence-gathering and counterintelligence capabilities, ensuring
that they were prepared for future threats.

### Economic Development and Player Activity

The economy of New Eden was similarly shaped by the activities and
interactions of its citizens. Being a player-driven economy, it was
subject to the same laws of supply and demand that governed real-world
economies. Organizations played especially important roles within this
system, controlling vast portions of the economy and manipulating prices
to obtain maximum gains. Many of New Eden's organizations were dedicated
to economic activities exclusively, focusing on the production and
distribution of goods and services. Some specialized in the creation and
manufacturing of various materials, while others were involved in
transport and trade.

With the development and proliferation of specialized organizations,
economic activity within New Eden grew exponentially, reaching levels
previously unseen in the history of the game. Large-scale trade
operations dominated the economy, controlling vast swaths of New Eden's
territory and exerting considerable influence over the game's political
landscape. Trade hubs and economic hotspots emerged throughout the
space, their popularity stemming from the convenience and profitability
of their location.

### The Formation of Community Culture

As New Eden's institutions and organizations continued to develop and
evolve, so did the culture surrounding them. Many organizations formed
unique sets of practices, regulations, and procedures, distinguishing
themselves from their competitors while uniting their members under a
single banner. The themes of economic activity, organization, and
competition became especially prominent, with many organizations using
them as the foundation of their institutional memory and cultural
legacy. Community members of these organizations shared stories of
victories and losses, passing on their legacy to newer generations and
introducing them to the rich cultural heritage of their organizations.

This institutional memory was preserved and propagated in a variety of
ways, often taking the form of legends familiar to all members of the
organization. Many of these stories took origin in the actions and deeds
of notable figures and events, serving to illustrate the values and
principles upheld by the organizations. The cultural practices and
traditions of many institutions became intertwined with their economic
activity, influencing the actions and decisions of their members. The
institutions of New Eden, both large and small, continued to operate
under the shared principles of competition, reputation, and
organization, shaping the culture and history of their members.

New Eden saw the emergence of diverse and influential organizations that
were united by the themes of competition, organization, and reputation.
These institutions developed unique cultural heritages while building
their influence and power within New Eden. Large-scale organizations
controlled the economy and the environment, influencing the lives of
many within the game. In their efforts to maximize their gains and
minimize the losses, many organizations became involved in the economic,
political, and military aspects of New Eden, wielding considerable power
and influence.

The institutions of New Eden built their reputations on the principles
of competition and organization. Some of them acquired notoriety due to
their involvement in espionage and security breaches, demonstrating the
weaknesses of their economic and organizational structures. The legacy
of these organizations lived on, influencing the decisions of many New
Eden residents and shaping the development of future institutions and
practices. These themes continued to define the development of the
organizations and structures of New Eden, influencing and directing the
choices and actions of many of its inhabitants.

Power and Competition: The Rise of Political Systems, 2009-2013

The Rise of Power Structures within New Eden

By 2009, New Eden had grown from a set of disorganized corporations and
alliances into a sophisticated ecosystem of entities capable of
governing territories, regulating economies, and exerting political
influence over the greater game universe. The structures that had
emerged throughout the previous years had evolved from mere social
experiments into actual political instruments of power.

The primary source of power in New Eden was not purely military,
although having a strong fleet was an advantage in any conflict.
Military might was only one element of a greater system, one which also
included economies, logistics, diplomacy, intelligence, and the
leadership abilities of the organizations behind the fleets. While a
powerful alliance might secure short-term victories, true dominance
required a deep underlying structure that could provide the military
might with the means and motivation to continue its growth and
development.

The period was one of the early emergence of a fully-fledged political
structure within the game. The large-scale alliances had grown
progressively stronger and more organized, transforming into true
governments that ruled the space within their territorial possession.
The alliances engaged in territorial disputes, regulated the economies
of their possessions, negotiated treaties and engagements with foreign
entities, and exerted diplomatic pressure on other organizations. The
anarchy of the early days of New Eden had largely dissipated, replaced
with a complex system of territorial control and governance. New Eden
had entered an era of great power politics.

The Nature of Leadership and Authority

The emergence of large-scale organizations also brought with it a
greater need for leadership, an aspect of governance that had previously
been largely unimportant in the early days of New Eden. One of the most
important aspects of leadership in large-scale alliances was the
necessity of the position as well as the ways in which it was justified
in the eyes of the public. The authority of a leader depended on the
perceived ability of said leader to represent and protect the interests
of the people they led as well as provide said people with opportunities
and choices. The weakening of the public image of a political figure
often resulted in the loss of authority as well as internal unrest.

Leadership was one of the most difficult professions within New Eden,
one that often required a great amount of dedication to the cause of
one's organization. While many people were capable of commanding a
large-scale army in battle, relatively few could dedicate enough time
and effort to maintaining and organizing large-scale organizations. The
position of a leader within an organization also varied in complexity.
Alliance directors and managers, for instance, were responsible for a
large part of the financial and military management of their respective
alliances, as well as the coordination and regulation of their
organizational capacities. Alliances also required people skilled in
negotiating treaties, maintaining diplomatic relations, and managing the
internal conflicts of the alliances.

The Importance of Diplomacy

Large-scale alliances and organizations were rarely involved in direct
combat with each other. Instead, they maintained a system of diplomacy
that permitted them to establish alliances, engage in territorial
disputes, and wage wars against each other. Diplomacy served as the
cornerstone of all large-scale political confrontations in New Eden, as
the nature of such conflicts precluded the involvement of a single
military entity. Diplomacy dictated the terms of engagement, treaties,
economic agreements and, in some cases, even wars.

Diplomacy also facilitated the establishment and maintenance of
relationships between various organizations and entities, whether
hostile, allied, or neutral. In many ways, it was through diplomacy that
alliances formed and dissolved, as well as engaged in negotiations with
each other. The art of diplomacy was a complex and evolving area, one
which saw shifting alliances between the various large-scale
organizations. Alliances of convenience were formed on a regular basis
in order to confront mutual enemies. However, the same alliances would
often turn against each other at the most opportune moments, shifting
their resources and manpower for greater strategic gains.

The Nature of Coalitions

The rise of large-scale organizations saw the rise of coalitions. The
growing competition between alliances necessitated the formation of
larger structures able to withstand the pressures of intense economic
competition and prolonged military conflicts. The alliances that formed
the foundations of New Eden's great power political system began to
enter the battlefield as true superpowers, their actions shaping and
defining the future conflict for the next several years.

Coalitions facilitated a different kind of warfare, one which involved a
far greater number of entities and players. The alliances and
organizations of the era had to mobilize vast amounts of resources,
manpower, and economic investment in order to fund large-scale
operations. The art of war had changed drastically, with large-scale
operations involving a far greater degree of logistics and strategy. The
military effectiveness of a given force in New Eden was no longer
strictly dependent on its combat power but rather the economic
capabilities of its coalition partners.

The Importance of Information

The information economy played a very important role in the development
of the political landscape of New Eden. Alliances invested a large
amount of resources in intelligence and counter-intelligence in order to
gain an advantage against their political and military rivals.
Information, rumors, and intelligence were valuable assets, often used
to determine the outcomes of political and military conflicts. Some of
the most powerful organizations of the era had entire departments
dedicated to the accumulation and analysis of intelligence data and
information regarding their competitors. The art of manipulation also
played a crucial role in the dissemination of misleading information and
rumors, as well as the use of propaganda to shape the public opinion of
the greater populace.

The ability to manipulate the perception of reality was one of the most
crucial skills needed by every political entity. Organizations and
alliances had to dedicate vast amounts of resources and manpower to
managing their public image while also investing resources into
obtaining the intelligence data needed to make informed decisions
regarding their future strategies and options.

The Invisible Infrastructure of New Eden Society

Despite the immense scale of economic and military power wielded by the
organizations of the era, the functioning of New Eden still depended on
individuals and their contributions. The large-scale alliances and
corporations of the era were dependent on the efforts of thousands of
individuals who worked tirelessly in order to ensure the continued
prosperity and growth of their organizations.

The efforts of these people, however, seldom earned them any rewards,
with the majority of the benefits reaped by the corporate upper
management. The individuals who worked for the corporations and
alliances were often tasked with menial duties, such as managing
spreadsheets, fleets, and other organizational tools that comprised the
framework of the organization. While the efforts of these people were
vital to the success of the corporations, most of them did not dedicate
their lives to the cause entirely, often spending their time on other
activities when not working for the corporations.

The reliance of New Eden's economic and political system on volunteers
was an important insight into the nature of the structures and
institutions of the greater system. The majority of the infrastructure
of New Eden economy and politics existed outside the game, often in the
form of people who had dedicated countless hours and effort to
maintaining the game economy. For most of these people, the game was
only a small part of their lives, although the rest of their lives were
often devoted to the game entirely.

This period of New Eden history showed that large-scale organizations
and institutions mirrored many of the traits found in real-life
institutions. The organizations possessed an internal structure that had
to be carefully maintained in order to ensure stability. Large-scale
alliances and corporations were reliant on volunteers who dedicated a
large amount of effort to the organizations. These institutions also
struggled with the issues of leadership and management, as well as the
importance of economic, military, and intelligence investment in the
development of the organization as well as its long-term growth and
sustainability.

The Formation of New Eden's Political Structure

New Eden had entered a new era of territorial disputes, military
conflicts, and competition between powerful alliances and corporations.
The large-scale entities which had emerged from the chaos of New Eden's
early years dominated the political and economic spheres of the game
world. The alliances fought each other using their vast military,
economic, and intelligence resources, with the most powerful entities
shaping the future of the entire system.

The period saw the development of New Eden's political system and the
emergence of institutions which would dominate the landscape of the
game's political landscape for years to come. It was also a period
characterized by an evolution in the nature of large-scale warfare and
the role of military power in general.

The most important concept to emerge from the developments of the era
was the change in the nature of competition and the shift towards
institutional confrontation. New Eden had entered the realm of
institutional politics, with political entities competing with each
other through their organizational power and capabilities. The
organizations had to evolve in order to keep up with the changing needs
and demands of their members and the world around them.

Crisis and Transformation: The Changing Structure of New Eden, 2013-2017

A Period of Adjustment and Structural Change

The developments which took place in New Eden in 2013 represented the
transition towards a new stage in the development of the system. The
alliances and large-scale corporations which had defined the landscape
of New Eden for the past decade had entered a period of slow decline as
new organizations arose to challenge their dominance. The old structures
were no longer as effective as they once were, as their reliance on
massive organizational capacity and economic might made them unwieldy
and difficult to control.

The changes which took place between 2013 and 2017 reflected an
adjustment period for New Eden as a whole. The system had to adapt to
new realities, as the institutions which had previously been the driving
force behind the economy and politics of New Eden were no longer able to
fulfill the changing demands of their constituents.

The Shift in Competitive Dynamics and Nature of Conflict

The competitive dynamics of New Eden had largely been dictated by the
nature of the alliances and corporations. The early years of the game
saw the creation of large-scale structures which facilitated territorial
expansion and the acquisition of resources by their members. The
corporations and alliances of the era competed against each other in
order to secure the most lucrative possessions and gain dominance over
the most valuable systems.

The competition took on a different nature in the later years as the
organizations grew stronger and more sophisticated. New alliances and
corporations had to adapt to the new realities, as the old principles of
engagement no longer produced results. While the organizations continued
to compete against each other, the nature of political and military
confrontations had shifted.

The organizations were now so large and complex that they could no
longer rely on their economic and military might alone. Coordinating
thousands of players under a single banner was a difficult endeavor, as
there were a number of different factors which had to be taken into
account. Large-scale alliances, for instance, had to dedicate vast
resources into maintaining their own internal economy, security, and
logistics. The nature of warfare had also changed, as large alliances
and corporations had to prepare extensively before any military
engagement in order to maximize their chances of success.

While the organizations of the era had the ability to launch large-scale
attacks, their combined economic and military might often proved to be a
disadvantage, as their sheer size often made them unwieldy. The
organizations which had previously dominated the game were no longer
able to keep up with the increased tempo of operations, which
represented a fundamental shift in the nature of competition and
conflict. The large alliances and corporations were no longer as
effective at maintaining their dominance as before, with the majority of
them only able to sustain their status as dominant powers for a
relatively short period of time.

Challenges of Sustaining Dominance and Authority

The large-scale organizations of the era had to deal with an additional
challenge, as they were no longer able to rely on their internal
strength alone. The organizations had to secure their holdings and
maintain their power, as the increased complexity of the system and
competition made their dominance a precarious position. New alliances
and corporations often challenged the authority of the established
powers for the most desirable possessions in the game, including
systems, trade routes, and other resources.

The ability of the large alliances and corporations to hold their ground
and maintain their status was a matter of their strategic and economic
position. In many cases, such organizations had to maintain a delicate
balance, as their ability to provide security and opportunities for
their members was the main factor in their continued success. Any
failure to meet the demands of one's constituents often led to the rapid
dissolution of the organization. The alliances and corporations of the
era were dependent on the loyalty and commitment of their members, who
were often unwilling to dedicate their time and effort to a failing
cause.

A similar set of circumstances also applied to the organizational
leadership, who had to manage a number of difficult tasks. The increased
complexity of operations meant that the directors and managers of large
alliances and corporations spent the majority of their time on
administrative duties. The directors of the large-scale organizations
were responsible for a number of factors, including the financial
stability of their organizations as well as their military readiness and
organizational capacity. The directors were often the sole figures
responsible for the decision-making process, with very few able to
participate in the actual governance of the organization.

The Relationship between Organizations and Their Constituents

The organizations of the era continued to rely on the same principles
which had allowed them to thrive in previous years. The alliances and
corporations competed against each other in order to gain the favor of
the populace, as the support of the citizens was the most valuable asset
in New Eden. Organizations had to dedicate significant resources to
maintaining their public image and managing their reputation, as it
played an important role in their ability to attract new members.

A strong reputation could greatly enhance the influence of the
organization, allowing it to attract additional resources and manpower.
Even the victories of the organizations could be undermined by poor
public perception, which could limit their ability to conduct further
operations. As a result, the organizations had to dedicate substantial
resources to managing their public image and reputation. In that sense,
the organization's public perception was one of the most crucial
elements of their ability to survive and thrive in New Eden.

The Importance of Economic Sustainability

While the large alliances and corporations continued to play a crucial
role in the system, their influence was no longer as overwhelming as it
used to be. The organizations now relied on economic sustainability far
more than before, as their internal logistics and infrastructure had
become extremely complex. The majority of large alliances and
corporations invested a significant amount of resources into their own
production and economy, which in turn allowed them to fund their
military operations and other activities.

The competition between the organizations for economic stability was the
central factor in the development of the game's economy and politics.
The corporations and alliances engaged in a number of economic
confrontations as they vied for the most valuable assets and competitive
advantages. The organizations which were unable to achieve economic
sustainability often found themselves unable to compete against their
rivals, resulting in their eventual downfall.

The organizations and alliances of the era once again demonstrated that
the success of large-scale entities in New Eden was dependent on
elements far beyond military might. The alliances and corporations of
the era continued to rely on the support of their constituents, with
their survival often dependent on their ability to fulfill the needs and
desires of their members. The ability of an organization to maintain its
image and reputation played an equally important role in its longevity.
The organizations which were unable to adapt to the new realities of the
system were swiftly overtaken by their more prepared rivals.

The Rise of New Players and the Evolution of Generational Dynamics

One important characteristic of the developments which took place in New
Eden between 2013 and 2017 was the emergence of new players. The game
had experienced a wave of popularity throughout the years, with
thousands of new players joining the system every year. The influx of
new blood resulted in large-scale shifts, as many of the new players
came to dominate the game's competitive arena.

The new players were not only numerous, but they also often arrived in
large groups. Such players were frequently far more disciplined and
organized than the veteran members of the game, as their combined
efforts allowed them to overcome the established organizations. The
veterans who had ruled New Eden for years were often unable to ignore
the rising wave of newcomers, who were now far more numerous and
stronger.

Such developments represented a fundamental shift in the nature of
competition within New Eden, as the players who had previously dominated
the system were now forced to compete against far more organized and
determined opponents. For many veterans, the arrival of the newcomers
represented a severe challenge, as they now had to adjust to the new
dynamics of the game in order to remain competitive. At the same time,
the new generations of players often struggled to adapt to the realities
of the system and its complex structures.

The new players of the era were often far less prepared to dedicate time
and effort to the game than the veterans. While it took considerable
effort to build an organization from the ground up, the majority of the
new players were only willing to dedicate limited resources to the
endeavor. As a result, many of the newcomers were only able to sustain
their presence within the system for a short period of time. The influx
of new players and their failure to adapt to the game was yet another
factor which contributed to the decline of the large-scale alliances and
corporations of the era.

Transformation and the End of an Era

The developments which took place between 2013 and 2017 represented one
of the most pivotal moments in the history of New Eden. The era was
defined by the transformations which took place within the game's
institutions, as well as the shifting dynamics of competition.
Organizations which had previously been dominant for years were no
longer able to sustain their presence in the system, while new alliances
and corporations arose to take their place.

The new era also saw the rise of a new generation of players who were
much better prepared to compete against their adversaries. The newcomers
possessed greater levels of discipline and organization, which allowed
them to overcome the previously-dominant entities. While many veterans
continued to compete after their initial defeats, they were largely
unable to withstand the organized assaults of the newcomers, and were
eventually forced to concede the battlefield.

This period represented a time of transformation for New Eden, as the
game and its players had to adjust to new realities. The game had become
far more competitive, and the organizations and alliances which ruled it
had to evolve in order to survive.

Continuity and Change: The Survival of New Eden, 2017--Present

A Mature Digital Society

By 2017, New Eden had already existed for over a decade. The
experimental chaos of its infancy had passed, political structures had
crystallized, and a competent leadership class had emerged. The
challenges facing the players were no longer centered on the fundamental
possibility of institutionalization, but rather on adapting to a
changing environment while retaining existing structures.

While many of the early organizations had been dissolved, their legacy
persisted. New organizations were formed with an eye toward past
conflicts, and new leaders learned their lessons from their
predecessors. The history of New Eden was no longer an abstraction, but
rather a resource to be leveraged.

The community persisted and evolved, with organizations that failed to
adapt to the changing environment being replaced by those that were more
innovative and less conservative.

The Importance of Historical Memory

One particular feature of New Eden's development was its complex
relationship with its own past. The community's history was an integral

This shift in the relationship between generations was an important part
